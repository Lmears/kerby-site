Change it while it plays.

Turn the Rate knobs to change the rhythm, or try the shortcuts below.

  • Keep it playing hands-free. Press a key to change the note.

  • Tap for one hit. While playing, tap to mute or bring it back.

  • Mangle the pulse on the grid. Keep pressing to cycle back to off.

  • Bend both voices a semitone at a time.

  • The bass ducks under every kick. Switch it off to hear the difference.

In phase on every note.

Never fix phase again. Auto-align lines your bass up with the kick on every hit, so the low end stays full even when the note changes. On by default.

Hold a note. Or place every hit.

Play a pattern

Hold a MIDI note and KerBy repeats it as kick and bass pulses, in time with your DAW. That’s what the keys here do.

Write it in

Write the rhythm in your DAW’s piano roll. Each MIDI note triggers both kick and bass.

Draw a wave. Drop a sample.

Shape each hit with amplitude, pitch and filter curves. The kick’s Click layer adds a sampled attack on top.

Push it further with FM, saturation, noise and downsampling, or a low rumble from the kick’s reverb.

Drag a bar from any scope straight into your DAW as audio, or back onto a Wave tab.

Sidechain, built in.

The bass ducks under every kick, so the two never fight over the low end. On by default.

DucKy, a free ducking plugin, ducks any other track or group to KerBy’s kick.

Macros, LFOs and your own shapes.

One macro can reshape the kick and bass together. Lock both voices to a scale, then route a random LFO to the bass’s Tune for a bassline that stays in key.

The voices are called Kick and Bass, but the sounds and patterns can go well beyond either.
